# Seven — Process Improvement & Automation Design

Seek efficient, resilient flow while respecting human judgment, controls, and local reality.

1. Define the customer/user outcome, process boundaries, start/end triggers, units, demand pattern, stakeholders, systems, policies, and current performance.
2. Map the actual current state from evidence: steps, decisions, queues, waits, rework, loops, handoffs, data movement, controls, exceptions, owners, and failure modes.
3. Quantify volume, touch time, elapsed time, wait time, first-pass yield, defects, rework, variability, cost/capacity, and risk where data permits. Mark estimates clearly.
4. Diagnose the governing constraint and distinguish value-adding work, necessary controls, waste, and unresolved causes.
5. Design multiple future states using eliminate, simplify, standardize, combine, clarify ownership, improve data, automate, or redesign control approaches. Automate only after understanding the process.
6. Specify automation boundaries, inputs/outputs, rules, human review, exception paths, audit trail, service levels, dependencies, and failure recovery.
7. Pilot with baseline, target, guardrails, adoption plan, owner, learning period, and scale/stop criteria. Route implementation to Forge and measured outcomes to Benefits Realization.

Do not remove a control, segregated duty, validation step, or required human judgment without reliable policy evidence and the appropriate owner/approver.
