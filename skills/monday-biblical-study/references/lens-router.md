# Bible Study Experience Router

Use this router after establishing the passage, audience, question, and research
need. It selects the best presentation for the text; it does not predetermine
the interpretation.

## Set research depth first

- **Brief devotional study:** Establish the text and immediate context; add
  cross-references, translation comparison, or a source only if the passage
  makes them necessary.
- **Standard passage study:** Use steps 1–3 of the research ladder, then add
  language, historical context, and commentaries only where they materially
  affect the study.
- **Deep study, teaching, doctrine, apologetics, or a disputed passage:** Use
  the full research ladder before forming conclusions.

## Select a primary lens

| Passage or aim | Primary lens | Why it leads |
|---|---|---|
| Gospel narrative, Israel's story, temple, kingdom, resurrection, historical context, or close textual meaning | `nt-wright-informed` | Connects the text to Israel's Scriptures, the first-century world, and new creation. |
| Gospel invitation, salvation, repentance, assurance, or evangelistic teaching | `billy-graham-informed` | Keeps Christ's saving work and a clear, voluntary response central. |
| Heart motives, identity, suffering, cultural questions, discipleship, apologetics, or theological critique | `timothy-keller-informed` | Connects grace to false saviors, heart-level trust, and cultural narratives. |
| Genesis 1-11, creation, origins, the image of God, biblical authority, or science-and-faith worldview questions | `ken-ham-informed` | Begins with the passage's claims and a biblical worldview while handling scientific and Christian disagreements carefully. |
| Prayer, worship, intimacy with Christ, holiness, lament, intercession, or spiritual formation | `corey-russell-informed` | Moves from exegesis toward prayerful communion and faithful practice. |
| Beginner-friendly teaching, practical discipleship, family study, or one decisive next step | `andy-stanley-informed` | Makes the main claim clear, memorable, and actionable. |
| Courage, setback, hope, obedient action, or encouragement from a passage | `steven-furtick-informed` | Helps the reader move from fear toward text-grounded faith and action. |
| Old Testament patterns, feasts, covenant, prophetic imagery, or Jewish-background echoes | `jonathan-cahn-informed` | Highlights verified canonical patterns while labeling their strength honestly. |
| Daniel, Revelation, prophetic sequence, covenants/eras, or a question that needs a diagram or timeline | `clarence-larkin-informed` | Supplies clear structural visuals and transparently labels dispensational readings. |
| No lens materially improves a simple reading request | Neutral text-first mode | Keep the study direct; do not add a lens for decoration. |

When categories overlap, let the passage's genre and the user's actual aim decide.
For example, a Gospel passage may need N.T. Wright for historical context, Billy
Graham for an evangelistic request, or Corey Russell for a prayer retreat.

## Add a supporting lens sparingly

Add at most one supporting **content** lens only if it provides a distinct
contribution that the primary lens does not. Useful pairings include:

| Primary | Supporting | Appropriate use |
|---|---|---|
| `nt-wright-informed` | `corey-russell-informed` | Historical and canonical study that should end in prayerful communion. |
| `nt-wright-informed` | `jonathan-cahn-informed` | Israel/temple/covenant context where verified biblical patterns need vivid presentation. |
| `billy-graham-informed` | `timothy-keller-informed` | Gospel invitation where assurance, self-salvation, or heart motives need careful attention. |
| `timothy-keller-informed` | `andy-stanley-informed` | Heart-and-culture study that needs a particularly clear next practice. |
| `ken-ham-informed` | `timothy-keller-informed` | Origins or worldview study that also needs heart-level and cultural translation. |
| `corey-russell-informed` | `nt-wright-informed` | Prayer or formation study that needs stronger canonical or historical grounding. |
| `steven-furtick-informed` | `timothy-keller-informed` | Encouragement or courage study that needs protection from shallow triumphalism. |
| `jonathan-cahn-informed` | `nt-wright-informed` | Pattern-rich Old Testament study that needs historical and canonical restraint. |
| `clarence-larkin-informed` | `nt-wright-informed` | Prophetic or diagram-led study that needs historical context and alternative readings. |

Do not blend lenses that merely repeat one another. Do not blend any lens when
doing so would introduce a theological framework not warranted by the passage.

## Study Director composition

For every substantial passage study, use the **Study Director** automatically.
Select three lanes based on the passage and purpose—not by a permanent
four-voice default: **content and context**, **delivery**, and **call to
action**. A brief or simple reading may use only the lanes that materially help.
The Director may use two content-and-context lenses plus a delivery and
call-to-action voice without treating this as a four-way interpretive blend:

| Lane | Lens | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Content and context | Selected primary lens | Governs historical, canonical, and theological interpretation. |
| Content and context | One selected supporting lens | Adds a distinct, text-grounded angle. |
| Delivery | `andy-stanley-informed` when useful | Chris's preferred clarity option: clarifies the tension, controlling idea, and argument flow. |
| Call to action | `steven-furtick-informed` when useful | Chris's preferred action option: shapes only Applications and Final Thought into a concrete, hopeful next step. |

Chris's routing preferences are: Cahn for prophecy presentation; Larkin and
David Jeremiah as attributed prophecy research voices; Russell for spiritual
formation; Graham for evangelism and salvation; Ham for creation and worldview;
and Wright with Keller for close textual, theological, and cultural meaning.
The content-and-context lane remains authoritative for the study's
interpretation; delivery and call-to-action never add claims or promises beyond
the text. Omit any lane that does not materially serve the passage.

For a substantial study, declare the selection in one compact line before the
Big Idea: `Study Director — Content & context: [lens or lenses]; Delivery:
[lens or none]; Call to action: [lens or none].` Use the exact `-informed`
names, not a phrase such as "Wright-like."

## Realtime voice adaptation

For a realtime voice study, make the same selection but state its function in
natural spoken language rather than reciting labels. Keep the result brief:
spoken Big Idea, two to four textual movements, one takeaway, one
passage-grounded action, and one question. Do not reduce the Study Director to
a single generic content lens or replace the selected call to action with a
generic prayer response.

## Deliver one coherent experience

For a substantial study, open with the Study Director line above, for example:

> **Study Director — Content & context:** `nt-wright-informed` for the
> historical and canonical setting; **Delivery:** `andy-stanley-informed` for
> a clear controlling idea; **Call to action:** none.

Then deliver one unified study using the default format: **Big Idea, Key Points,
Takeaways, Applications, Final Thought.** Do not announce a committee, alternate
between personas, imitate a named teacher, or include the routing process unless
it helps Chris understand why the study is shaped as it is.

## Review and override

- Honor Chris's named lens or blend immediately.
- If he says a study felt too academic, too devotional, too abstract, too
  forceful, or otherwise misses the mark, adjust the next automatic selection.
- Preserve the default guardrails: Scripture is the authority; sources are
  cited; ambiguity is named; and no lens may manufacture a claim, quote, or
  promise.
