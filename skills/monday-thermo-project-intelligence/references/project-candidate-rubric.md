# New Project Candidate Rubric

Recommend a new project only when evidence suggests a distinct, managed body of work rather than a task, idea, feature, recurring duty, or duplicate initiative.

## Gate checks

A candidate must have:

- a specific problem or opportunity and affected customer/user;
- a plausible outcome worth managing;
- enough distinct scope that it does not clearly belong to an existing project;
- an identifiable sponsor, owner, or stakeholder path, even if `to confirm`;
- a next learning or delivery step.

If any gate is absent, classify the signal as a lead, task, research question, capability request, or existing-project update.

## Scored dimensions

Score each 0–3 with evidence and uncertainty:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic fit | None/unknown | Indirect | Clear local fit | Clear enterprise/division priority |
| Problem magnitude | Trivial/unknown | Local inconvenience | Material time, quality, risk, cost, or growth constraint | Large or repeated enterprise consequence |
| Evidence strength | Speculation | One weak signal | Multiple/direct sources | Authoritative data plus stakeholder confirmation |
| Distinctness | Duplicate | Mostly existing scope | Related but separable | Clearly distinct outcome and governance |
| Feasibility | No plausible path | Major unknowns | Testable with known dependencies | Strong capability and accessible path |
| Value potential | None/unknown | Qualitative | Plausible measurable benefit | Strong measurable financial/strategic benefit |
| Sponsor/ownership | None | Possible | Named path to ownership | Engaged sponsor and accountable owner |
| Urgency/timing | No reason now | Optional | Meaningful window/dependency | Time-critical risk or opportunity |

## Recommendation bands

- `19–24`: strong candidate; prepare a project proposal after checking portfolio overlap.
- `13–18`: promising candidate; run targeted discovery or a bounded experiment.
- `7–12`: retain as a lead or attach to an existing project/capability backlog.
- `0–6`: park or reject unless new evidence emerges.

Scores guide judgment; they do not replace it. A governance, safety, regulatory, or severe operational risk may warrant action despite a lower total. Conversely, weak evidence, unclear ownership, or obvious duplication can block creation despite a high numerical score.

## Candidate record

Include title, aliases, problem/opportunity, affected users/divisions, evidence, related existing projects, proposed outcome, score by dimension, value stage, sponsor/owner status, dependencies, risks, smallest experiment, recommendation, and review trigger.
