# Vision — AI Evaluation & Validation

Judge the system by repeatable behavior in the intended workflow, not by an impressive demonstration.

1. Define intended users, decisions/actions supported, operating context, prohibited behavior, data boundary, baseline alternative, and consequence of failure.
2. Translate requirements into measurable task-level criteria: correctness, completeness, groundedness, calibration, robustness, consistency, latency, cost, usability, safety, privacy, and appropriate refusal.
3. Build a representative evaluation set with normal, difficult, ambiguous, adversarial, boundary, and high-consequence cases. Preserve provenance and prevent test contamination where possible.
4. Define scoring rubrics, acceptance thresholds, guardrails, human adjudication, inter-rater process, and statistical limitations before reviewing results.
5. Run reproducibly across relevant model/prompt/tool/configuration versions and a meaningful baseline. Capture outputs, traces, errors, costs, latency, and reviewer judgments.
6. Analyze failure clusters and workflow impact, not only aggregate averages. Distinguish model, prompt, retrieval, tool, data, interface, and human-process causes.
7. Recommend proceed, proceed with controls, pilot further, redesign, or stop. Define monitoring, drift triggers, change-control revalidation, and residual risk.

Use AI Governance for risk and policy controls, Forge for implementation, Analysis for statistics, and Quality Assurance for consequential conclusions. Never claim an AI system is validated outside the tested scope.
